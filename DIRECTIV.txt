The implementation agent may recommend alternatives but must not independently authorize architecture changes, financial activity, production spending, or sensitive operations.

SECTION 01 — PRODUCT MISSION AND OPERATING MODES

01.1 Mission
Build a secure, auditable, scalable, cost-aware trading and mining intelligence platform combining deterministic execution, validated quantitative intelligence, specialized agents, and human-controlled operational authority.

01.2 Initial Product Scope
The initial platform shall support:
1. Cryptocurrency spot-market intelligence and trading.
2. Simulation and paper trading.
3. Mining profitability simulation.
4. Controlled future mining hardware and pool integration.
5. MacBook desktop application.
6. iPhone-compatible remote dashboard.
7. Cloud-based trading execution and scheduling.
8. Multi-tenant user and profile management.
9. Financial accounting, profit policies, and notifications.
10. Independent safety, security, audit, and compliance controls.
The architecture shall support future expansion into additional financial markets, but stocks, forex, derivatives, leverage, and other products are not authorized for initial live operation.

01.3 Operating Modes
SIMULATION: Historical or synthetic data without real financial orders.
PAPER: Simulated trading using approved market data or sandbox environments.
LIVE: Real financial execution through explicitly authorized providers after all technical, security, legal, and human approval gates pass.
All interfaces, records, and audit events must clearly identify the active mode. A mode change must never silently enable real-money trading.

01.4 Scale Objective
The platform shall be designed for multi-tenant operation from inception.
The target of 10,000 users is a capacity-planning objective, not a verified claim of concurrent trading capacity. Performance and infrastructure requirements must be validated through realistic workload modeling and benchmark evidence.

SECTION 02 — APPROVED ARCHITECTURE DECISION REGISTER

All decisions below are binding at the level of their stated requirements.

AD-001 — Initial Domain Scope
Cryptocurrency spot trading and mining are the initial domains. Future markets require separate approval.

AD-002 — Multi-Tenant Foundation
Tenant isolation, authorization, quotas, and scalable service boundaries must be designed from the beginning.

AD-003 — Hybrid Low-Token Intelligence
Routine financial processing shall use deterministic algorithms and validated quantitative methods. LLM usage must remain bounded and nonauthoritative for execution.

AD-004 — Four Agents and Independent Safety Kernel
Implement exactly four primary specialized agents plus a separate deterministic Risk & Safety Kernel. The kernel is not a fifth agent.

AD-005 — Multi-Provider Financial Connectivity
Use approved provider adapters for exchange, wallet, and relevant financial connections. Maintain non-custodial boundaries, reconciliation, scoped credentials, and manual recovery after critical failures.

AD-006 — Cloud Execution and Calendar Sessions
Cloud services execute and control scheduled sessions independently of the MacBook or iPhone. Every session requires authorization and preflight checks.

AD-007 — Profit and Financial Governance
Support realized net-profit calculations, configurable profit thresholds, profit reservations, and controlled withdrawal-request workflows.

AD-008 — User Onboarding and Profiles
Support identity, jurisdiction, timezone, language, exchange connections, trading capital, risk policies, strategy configuration, and security preferences. User-entered profile information is not equivalent to provider KYC.

AD-009 — Notifications and Mobile Monitoring
Provide a unified notification hub, auditable transaction history, configurable profit alerts, and secure iPhone monitoring.

AD-010 — Zero-Trust Security
Use strong authentication, MFA, least privilege, secure credentials, tenant isolation, and independently enforced safety policies.

AD-011 — Quantitative and Validated ML Intelligence
Use deterministic market calculations, validated ML, and limited LLM analysis. LLM output must not directly authorize or execute orders.

AD-012 — Strategy Validation Pipeline
Require historical testing, realistic execution assumptions, out-of-sample analysis, walk-forward validation, paper trading, and controlled live-release gates.

AD-013 — Dynamic Risk Management
Enforce hard limits for exposure, daily loss, drawdown, concentration, volatility, liquidity, and correlated positions.

AD-014 — Deterministic Execution and Reconciliation
Use durable order state, unique identifiers, idempotency, explicit partial-fill handling, and reconciliation of uncertain exchange outcomes.

AD-015 — Mining Simulation and Integration
Begin with simulation and profitability validation. Real hardware and pool integration require explicit approval and safety controls.

AD-016 — Event-Driven Multi-Tenant Cloud
Use durable asynchronous processing, horizontally scalable workers, tenant isolation, and independent safety services.

AD-017 — Fault-Tolerant Infrastructure and Controlled Recovery
Services must recover without creating duplicate financial effects. Critical trading failures require SAFE_HALT, reconciliation, and human authorization before resumption.

AD-018 — Financial Event History and Multi-Level Profit Governance
Use durable, auditable financial records, balanced journal postings, exact decimal arithmetic, idempotent accounting, and configurable profit policies.
Full event sourcing across every service is not mandatory; the financial architecture must provide equivalent auditability and correctness guarantees.

AD-019 — Non-Custodial Credential Vault
Protect financial credentials through managed encryption, least privilege, tenant isolation, rotation, revocation, and audited access. Withdrawal-enabled trading credentials are prohibited by default.

AD-020 — Policy-Governed Agent Orchestration
Use typed agent contracts, explicit permissions, deterministic cross-verification, and independent risk authorization. Agent failures must not expand privileges.

AD-021 — Shared Market Data and Deterministic Signals
Normalize, validate, and reuse eligible public market data while isolating tenant-specific decisions and credentials. Reject stale or invalid market data from live execution.

AD-022 — Observability, Audit, and SRE Gates
Provide structured logs, distributed traces, operational metrics, tamper-evident audit records, incident evidence, and measurable service reliability objectives.

AD-023 — Digital Twin and Continuous Verification
Use deterministic simulation, exchange emulation, replay, fault injection, mining simulation, and repeatable verification before live operations.

AD-024 — Progressive Delivery and Safe Rollback
Use versioned artifacts, pinned dependencies, compatible migrations, staged releases, health gates, and safe rollback procedures. Rollback must not blindly reverse external financial effects.

AD-025 — Cost-Aware Orchestration
Enforce bounded LLM usage, tenant quotas, shared eligible data processing, rate limits, and infrastructure cost controls without weakening safety.

AD-026 — Centralized Identity and Step-Up Authorization
Enforce tenant-scoped identity, revocable sessions, MFA, and stronger authentication for sensitive operations.

AD-027 — Verified Remote Commands
Remote commands require typed action contracts, valid authorization, expiry, idempotency, and auditable outcomes.

AD-028 — Disaster Recovery
Provide multi-AZ resilience, tested backups, point-in-time recovery, execution ownership controls, and reconciliation before trading resumes.

AD-029 — Jurisdiction-Aware Compliance
Live functionality must be gated by provider eligibility, jurisdiction, product restrictions, and applicable legal obligations.

AD-030 — Unified MacBook and iPhone Experience
Provide consistent trading, mining, financial, scheduling, security, and incident-management interfaces backed by cloud authorization.

AD-031 — Technology Direction
Use Python/FastAPI for the core backend, React/TypeScript for interfaces, Tauri for the MacBook application, PostgreSQL for durable records, and managed cloud infrastructure. Specific messaging and cloud providers remain subject to evaluation.

AD-032 — Contract-First Human-Gated Development
Implement only authorized phases, verify outcomes, commit and push approved changes, produce evidence, and stop for human approval.

AD-033 — Architecture Audit and Readiness Gates
Architecture consistency, financial correctness, security, performance, compliance, and release readiness must be reviewed before production activation.

Decision Integrity
Every approved decision must map to implementation requirements, tests, and release evidence. No approved decision may be silently removed or contradicted.

SECTION 04 — FOUR SPECIALIZED AGENTS

04.1 Market Intelligence Agent
Responsibilities: Market observations; feature computation; market-condition analysis; bounded research summaries.
Prohibited: Direct trade execution.

04.2 Strategy & Portfolio Agent
Responsibilities: Strategy evaluation; portfolio analysis; position proposals; versioned trade intents.
Prohibited: Risk override or self-authorized execution.

04.3 Execution & Supervisor Agent
Responsibilities: Authorized order lifecycle coordination; exchange adapter orchestration; execution-state monitoring; reconciliation coordination.
Prohibited: Bypassing independent risk authorization.

04.4 Mining Operations Agent
Responsibilities: Mining simulation; profitability assessment; pool/hardware integration coordination; safety telemetry and reporting.
Prohibited: Unrestricted trading-account access.

04.5 Independent Risk & Safety Kernel
The Risk Kernel is a deterministic safety service, not an agent.
It validates tenant and profile authorization; session state; venue and instrument eligibility; capital and exposure; daily loss and drawdown; concentration and correlation; liquidity and volatility; market-data freshness; emergency-stop state; risk policy version; order-specific authorization validity.
Invariant: No exposure-increasing order may reach an exchange adapter without valid risk authorization.

SECTION 05 — TRADING EXECUTION CONTRACT

05.1 Mandatory Execution Pipeline
1. Validate market data.
2. Generate trade intent.
3. Verify tenant and session authorization.
4. Verify provider and compliance eligibility.
5. Reserve risk capacity.
6. Issue bounded risk authorization.
7. Persist the authorized order intent.
8. Submit through an approved exchange adapter.
9. Record acknowledgement or uncertain outcome.
10. Reconcile exchange execution.
11. Post confirmed financial effects.
12. Update audit and notifications.

05.2 Order States
CREATED; VALIDATED; RISK_APPROVED; SUBMISSION_PENDING; SUBMITTED; PARTIALLY_FILLED; FILLED; CANCELLED; REJECTED; EXPIRED; UNKNOWN_OUTCOME; RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED; SAFE_HALT.
All legal transitions must be defined and tested.

05.3 Idempotency
Every trade intent, order submission, fill, and financial posting must have a stable identity.
Timeouts must not trigger blind order resubmission. Unknown outcomes require exchange reconciliation. If safe reconciliation is impossible, new exposure-increasing operations must stop.

05.4 Risk Reservation and Concurrency
Concurrent strategies and sessions must not spend the same risk budget. Risk reservations must be concurrency-safe and tied to specific order parameters. Expired or materially changed authorizations must be rejected.

05.5 Exchange Adapters
Each approved adapter must define supported products and operations; authentication scopes; order submission and cancellation; order status and partial fills; client order identifiers; rate limits; protective order capabilities; recovery and reconciliation behavior; provider restrictions.
No exchange is automatically approved for live use.

05.6 Emergency Stop
Emergency stop must block new exposure-increasing orders; prevent automatic trading restart; preserve order and financial evidence; initiate reconciliation; apply only preauthorized protective actions; report actual exchange-side outcomes.
An accepted emergency command is not proof that all external orders were cancelled.

SECTION 06 — CLOUD SCHEDULER AND SESSION CONTROL

06.1 Scheduling
Support recurring and one-time sessions with explicit timezone handling.
Every session must specify tenant and profile; strategy version; exchange and instruments; capital allocation; risk policy; start and stop conditions; preflight requirements; end-of-session position policy.

06.2 Session States
DRAFT; SCHEDULED; PREFLIGHT; AWAITING_USER_APPROVAL; RUNNING; DEGRADED; SAFE_HALT; RECONCILING; COMPLETED; CANCELLED.

06.3 Session Safety
Scheduled events must not bypass authorization, compliance, or risk checks.
At normal session end, the default policy is to stop new entries and maintain approved protections for existing positions. Alternative closing policies require explicit authorization and risk validation.
Critical failures require reconciliation and human approval before resumption.

SECTION 07 — FINANCIAL LEDGER AND PROFIT MANAGEMENT

07.1 Ledger Principles
- Durable transactional records.
- Balanced financial postings.
- Exact decimal arithmetic.
- Asset-specific precision.
- Idempotent processing.
- Explicit correction entries.
- Reconciliation checkpoints.
- Traceable event provenance.

07.2 Financial Reporting
Distinguish realized net P&L; unrealized P&L; available trading balance; reserved capital; profit-lock reservations; provider-reported withdrawable balance; open liabilities and unsettled amounts.

07.3 Profit Policies
Support multiple configurable thresholds for profit alerts; profit reservations; trading stop conditions; withdrawal-request preparation; financial review.
Thresholds must not trigger duplicate financial effects.
Profit reservation is not a withdrawal. Actual withdrawals require separate authorization and provider/legal eligibility.

07.4 Reconciliation
Exchange-reported executions and balances must be reconciled with internal financial records. Discrepancies must produce explicit incidents or correction workflows. Silent ledger mutation is prohibited.

SECTION 08 — MARKET DATA AND INTELLIGENCE

08.1 Market Data Requirements

08.2 Shared Processing
Eligible public market data may be shared across tenants where provider terms permit. Tenant-specific strategies, credentials, positions, and decisions must remain isolated.

08.3 Intelligence Rules
Routine indicators and risk calculations must not depend on LLM availability. Validated ML may support research and market classification. LLM outputs must never be authoritative for prices, balances, order status, or financial permissions.

08.4 Strategy Validation

4. Out-of-sample evaluation.
5. Walk-forward testing.

7. Paper trading.

SECTION 09 — MINING OPERATIONS

09.2 Hardware and Pool Integration

SECTION 10 — SECURITY AND ZERO TRUST

10.1 Identity

10.2 Secrets
Use managed encryption and secrets infrastructure. Never store plaintext financial credentials in source code, Git, or ordinary logs. Default trading API credentials must not permit withdrawals.

10.3 Tenant Isolation
Enforce isolation at API boundary; service authorization; database access; event processing; secret access; financial records; agent context.
Cross-tenant access attempts must be tested and rejected.

10.4 Remote Action Contracts

Remote emergency stop must use a deterministic path independent of LLM processing.
